What's in moringa, and why it helps

Moringa, the drumstick tree, has been part of Indian kitchens for centuries. Its leaves are unusually nutrient-dense for a plant food, which is why it is often called a "miracle tree".

  • Fills nutrient gaps. Leaves supply iron, calcium, potassium and plant protein.
  • Supports immunity. Rich in vitamin C, and in vitamin A for eyes and skin.
  • Antioxidants. Polyphenols and flavonoids help the body handle everyday oxidative stress.
  • Good for the whole family. Easy to add to rotis, rice, chutneys and sambar.

Moringa is a nutritious food, not a medicine. Nutrient levels vary by preparation. If you have a health condition, are pregnant or take medication, check with your doctor.

Why tradition still matters

Sun-dried leaves, stone-and-spice blends, home-style pickles and papads: these methods came from generations of cooks who knew that simple ingredients handled patiently taste better and keep longer.

  • Whole ingredients, no shortcuts
  • Regional grains and lentils like horse gram and flax seed
  • Small batches prepared with hygiene and care
  • Fair income for farmers and rural women
